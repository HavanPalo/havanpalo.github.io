The short version

One notation, so everyone stops rebuilding the pipe

Every sensor, engine, and control system on a modern vessel produces data. The problem was never generating it — it was getting it off the ship in a form anyone downstream could actually trust: consistent timestamps, preserved quality codes, no silent gaps, no vendor lock-in.

D-INF doesn't certify your sensors or your analytics. It certifies the infrastructure in between — the vessel server that buffers it, the relay that moves it, and the shore server that stores it — so that whoever builds on top of that data isn't building on sand.

Three qualifiers cover three levels of ambition: a fully custom pipe for one use case, a flexible but proprietary one, or one built to a published standard that any compliant system can plug into.

The actual question

Why would anyone bother doing this?

Certifying a data pipe sounds like paperwork for its own sake. It isn't — here's what it's actually buying a shipowner or yard.

01

It's the on-ramp to cheaper surveys

Remote and data-driven verification (DDV) — annual trials done from shore instead of a surveyor flying out — depends on trustworthy data. D-INF is the prerequisite most of those programs are quietly built on top of.

02

It stops you rebuilding the pipe per vendor

Without a standard, every OEM ships its own proprietary data format. A standardized (S) infrastructure means a new sensor, engine, or analytics platform can plug into what's already there instead of triggering a fresh integration project.

03

It's what makes fleet-wide analytics possible

Predictive maintenance, fuel optimization, emissions reporting — all of it needs consistent, gap-free, correctly time-stamped data across every ship in the fleet, not just the newest one.

04

It's cheap to get, expensive to skip

Data quality management and cyber security management stay optional until you actually need them for another notation. You're not signing up for the whole stack — just the plumbing, now, while it's simple to specify at newbuild.

i

The honest counterpoint: if your vessel only ever needs one bespoke integration for one use case, the Essential qualifier — or no notation at all — may genuinely be enough. D-INF earns its cost when data needs to outlive the system that first collected it.
